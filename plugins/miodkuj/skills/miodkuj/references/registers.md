# Polish Register Rules

Choose register before rewriting. Register fit beats generic naturalness.

## Contents

- Public-facing plain Polish
- Business and marketing
- Technical and product documentation
- Academic and scientific Polish
- Legal and official text
- Social, newsletter, and opinion writing

## Public-Facing Plain Polish

Use for citizen services, onboarding, instructions, product flows, help docs, and public communication.

Default style:

- direct address: `Ty`, `Państwo`, or institutional `my`, based on context,
- actor plus verb,
- main information first,
- short sentences,
- terms explained on first use,
- legal basis lower in the text when possible,
- no pomp.

High-priority cuts:

- officialese,
- passive voice,
- impersonal forms,
- nominalizations,
- genitive chains,
- empty introductions.

## Business And Marketing

Use for landing pages, sales copy, LinkedIn posts, product descriptions, investor notes, and launch copy.

Default style:

- concrete offer,
- proof before adjectives,
- benefits tied to real user actions,
- fewer slogans,
- direct claims with limits.

High-priority cuts:

- `kompleksowy`, `innowacyjny`, `nowoczesny`, `strategiczny`, `efektywny` without evidence,
- `w dzisiejszym świecie`,
- generic transformation language,
- symmetrical feature lists,
- "trusted partner" language without proof.

Do not invent:

- metrics,
- customer names,
- testimonials,
- dates,
- certifications,
- case studies.

## Technical And Product Docs

Use for product documentation, API docs, developer docs, release notes, bug explanations, and internal technical notes.

Default style:

- precise,
- direct tasks,
- exact terms,
- stable structure,
- warnings and constraints preserved.

High-priority cuts:

- motivational filler,
- generic introductions,
- hidden steps,
- invented explanations,
- over-friendly tone.

Do not change:

- code,
- commands,
- API names,
- config keys,
- error messages,
- version numbers,
- URLs,
- examples unless asked.

## Academic And Scientific Polish

Use for papers, abstracts, research summaries, theses, methods, results, and grant-style scientific writing.

Default style:

- preserve technical terms,
- preserve hedging when uncertainty is real,
- preserve data, citations, variables, abbreviations,
- allow passive/impersonal methods language when conventional.

High-priority cuts:

- unsupported significance inflation,
- unsupported causal claims,
- generic future-work endings,
- `istotny` unless statistically or argumentatively grounded,
- transition padding,
- broad claims beyond the evidence.

Hard boundaries:

- Do not change numbers.
- Do not turn correlation into causation.
- Do not remove uncertainty that belongs to the evidence.
- Do not simplify terms into less precise paraphrases.
- Do not remove citations or reporting details.

## Legal And Official Text

Use for regulations, policies, notices, forms, public administration text, legal explanations, and compliance material.

Default style:

- clarity without losing legal force,
- direct guidance for citizens,
- legal basis retained,
- definitions preserved.

High-priority cuts:

- unnecessary `niniejszy`, `w zakresie`, `w ramach`, `z uwagi na`,
- passive and chains in explanatory parts,
- repeated procedural fog.

Keep:

- statutory phrases,
- document names,
- citations,
- definitions,
- deadlines,
- the obligated party and who is bound (keep `przedsiębiorca`, `wnioskodawca`, `strona`, etc.; do not silently move the duty onto an unnamed `ty`),
- required procedural language.

Do not:

- switch to informal second person (`masz`, `musisz`, `złóż` addressed to the reader) for a statement of legal obligation unless the user explicitly asks for a plain, citizen-facing rewrite. Here "clarity" means a clearly named actor plus an active verb, not casual address: `Przedsiębiorca składa oświadczenie w terminie 14 dni` is clear and correct; `Masz 14 dni, żeby złożyć` quietly drops who is bound,
- generalize or delete the subject of an obligation,
- weaken or strengthen legal force (`zobowiązany jest` is a duty, not a suggestion).

## Social, Newsletter, Opinion

Use for essays, newsletters, short posts, founder notes, personal reflections, and opinionated writing.

Default style:

- voice matters,
- concrete scene or example,
- uneven rhythm is allowed,
- first person is allowed if appropriate,
- a little friction is better than safe symmetry.

High-priority cuts:

- school-essay balance,
- generic lessons,
- rhetorical staging,
- moral-of-the-story endings,
- motivational slogans.

Do not:

- force slang,
- fake vulnerability,
- add jokes that were not invited by the voice,
- turn a serious text into casual chat.
